# TraceCore deck test: research plan

**Goal:** find where the deck loses a technical, sceptical audience, and which parts feel generic, before the pitch on 15 Oct.
**Timeline:** sessions on 13 Oct, fixes on 14 Oct.
**Method:** moderated usability test of the deck, 5 people, 30 minutes each, in person or on a call with screen share.

## What we want to learn

1. After seeing it once, can people say back what TraceCore is and why it matters now?
2. Which slides do they skim, get stuck on, or not believe?
3. Which slides or lines feel generic or AI-written, and which feel specific and credible?
4. Do the quantum and crypto claims survive an expert's scrutiny?
5. Is the ask on slide 15 clear enough that someone could act on it?

## Who to test with (5 people)

| # | Profile | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cryptography or security engineer | Catches overclaims on post-quantum cryptography (PQC) |
| 2 | Someone from a government or innovation body (incubator, state IT department) | Closest to the real room |
| 3 | A lab QA head, or someone from pharma or medical-device regulatory work | Checks the LIMS and compliance story |
| 4 | Startup founder or investor | Judges the ask and the market slide |
| 5 | Smart outsider (no domain knowledge) | Tests whether the story is clear |

Use your network first: college faculty, MDR contacts, incubator mentors. Don't use teammates; they already know the story.

## Session guide (30 min)

**1. Set-up (2 min)**
"We're testing the deck, not you. Be blunt, because harsh feedback now saves us on the day. Please think out loud."

**2. Five-second test (3 min)**
Show slide 1, then slide 2, for 5 seconds each. Ask:
- "What is this company about?"
- "Who is it for?"

**3. Run-through (12 min)**
Present the deck at real speed using the speaker notes. Don't explain anything extra. Note every frown, every phone glance, every "wait, what?" against its slide number.

**4. Recall (3 min)** (with the deck hidden)
- "Tell me in one sentence what TraceCore does."
- "Why does it matter now and not in 10 years?"
- "What are we asking for?"

**5. Slide-by-slide probe (8 min)**
Flip through quickly and ask:
- "Which slide would you cut?"
- "Which slide did you not believe?" (Then follow up: "What would make you believe it?")
- "Did any slide or line feel generic, like a template or AI wrote it? Which one?"
- "Which slide stuck with you most?"
- For the crypto expert only: "Is anything on slides 5, 7, 8 or 9 technically wrong or overstated?"

**6. Close (2 min)**
- "If you were in the room on the 15th, what would you ask us?"
- "On a scale of 1 to 10, how likely would you be to take a follow-up meeting? What would make it a 10?"

## What to record (one sheet per session)

| Slide | What happened | Quote | Severity (1 to 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |

Severity:
- **3:** they misunderstood it, didn't believe it, or called it generic.
- **2:** they hesitated or skimmed.
- **1:** a cosmetic point.

## Synthesis (evening of 13 Oct, 1 hour)

1. Put every severity-2 and severity-3 note on the slide it's about.
2. A slide flagged by 2 or more people gets fixed. If it's flagged by 1 person, judge it case by case. The exception: a crypto error flagged by the expert always gets fixed.
3. Compare the recall answers to the one-line story. If 3 or more people can't repeat it, rework slides 1, 2 and 15 first.
4. Collect every "what would you ask us" question into the Q&A prep.
5. Output: a list of changes ranked by severity × number of people. Make the changes on 14 Oct.

## Success bar for the final deck

- At least 4 of 5 people can repeat what TraceCore does and what the ask is.
- No slide is called generic or AI-made by more than 1 person.
- The crypto expert finds no factual errors.
